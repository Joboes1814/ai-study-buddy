# AI Study Buddy 2.0 — Research Question

## Research Area

Human–AI Interaction, Responsible AI, and Artificial Intelligence in Education.

## Research Problem

AI-powered educational assistants can provide students with fast access to explanations, summaries, practice questions, and personalized academic support.

However, the usefulness of an AI assistant depends on more than the quality of the answer it produces.

Students must also decide whether they trust the response, understand where the information comes from, recognize the limitations of the AI system, and remain actively involved in their own learning.

Many AI systems provide answers without clearly showing the evidence used to generate them. This may encourage users to accept AI-generated information without sufficient evaluation.

AI Study Buddy 2.0 therefore explores whether greater transparency can improve the relationship between learners and AI systems.

## Primary Research Question

> **How do transparency and source-grounded explanations influence student trust and perceived usefulness in an AI learning assistant?**

## Supporting Questions

1. Do students report greater trust when AI-generated answers include identifiable supporting sources?

2. Does showing the evidence behind an AI answer improve perceived usefulness?

3. Does explaining why an answer was generated help students critically evaluate AI-generated information?

4. How does source transparency influence a student's willingness to rely on an AI assistant?

5. Do students prefer concise answers, detailed explanations, or adjustable levels of explanation?

6. How can an AI learning assistant preserve learner agency while still providing useful academic support?

## Preliminary Variables

### Possible Independent Variables

* Presence or absence of supporting sources
* Presence or absence of AI explanation
* Answer detail level
* Student control over response format

### Possible Dependent Variables

* Perceived trust
* Perceived usefulness
* Understanding
* Confidence in evaluating the answer
* Willingness to reuse the assistant
* Perceived learner control

## Preliminary Hypothesis

Students who receive AI-generated answers with clearly presented supporting evidence and explanations may report greater ability to evaluate those answers and may develop more appropriately calibrated trust than students who receive answers without supporting information.

This hypothesis is preliminary and may be revised as the research design develops.

## Research Contribution

This project aims to contribute to the broader discussion around responsible AI in education by examining how interface and explanation design can influence the relationship between learners and intelligent systems.

The project is especially interested in AI systems that support human reasoning rather than simply automate academic tasks.
